---
term: Self-hosting
summary: Running software on infrastructure you control, a home server or a rented box, instead of a managed third-party service.
related:
  - homelab
---

Self-hosting trades a subscription and someone else's operational burden for direct control over the data, the configuration, and the failure modes. What made it a niche hobby for a long time was the operational cost: patching, backups, and remote access used to require real systems administration skill.

Cheap hardware, tools like Tailscale that make secure remote access close to zero-configuration, and terminal-based coding agents that can debug a broken service at 2 a.m. have lowered that bar significantly. The trade-off that doesn't go away is that outages, backups, and security patches are now the host's problem, with no support line to call.
